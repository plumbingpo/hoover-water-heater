A tankless unit rethinks the whole idea of a water heater. There is no tank to keep hot, so there is no standby loss while you sleep or travel. When a faucet or shower opens, the unit senses flow and fires, heating water as it moves through a heat exchanger. The result is a continuous supply for as long as you stay within the unit's rating, a compact footprint on the wall, and a lifespan that can be nearly double that of a typical tank. Families across Hoover, AL often choose tankless during a replacement or remodel for exactly those reasons.

A good tankless install starts with math, not tools. We total your peak demand in gallons per minute, such as a shower, a kitchen sink, and the dishwasher at once, and compare it against how much the unit must heat cold winter water. That tells us the real capacity you need. Next we check the supply side. Gas tankless units fire at up to around 199,000 BTU, so a half-inch line that fed an old tank is often too small. Condensing models vent through PVC or polypropylene and need a condensate drain, while non-condensing units need stainless steel venting. Electric units bring their own demands, often needing multiple large breakers and sometimes a service upgrade.

Every tankless install from Hoover Water Heater includes isolation service valves so future descaling takes minutes instead of hours, proper gas piping with a drip leg, correctly pitched and terminated venting, and a pressure relief valve piped to a safe location. If your water is hard, we talk through scale protection, since mineral buildup is what shortens tankless life most. We can add recirculation for faster hot water at distant bathrooms, set the temperature, handle permits where required, and walk you through maintenance before we leave. Warning Signs to Watch For

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Renovation work that makes gas line and vent changes easier to fit in
  • Showers turning lukewarm before everyone gets a turn
  • An aging tank that has started to rust, rumble, or leak
  • A growing household or new bathroom adding hot water demand
  • Wanting a unit that can last up to twice as long as a typical tank
  • A utility area where a bulky tank takes up room you would rather use
  • Paying to keep a full tank hot even when no one is home
  • A distant bath or detached space that needs its own hot water source

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Tankless installation is more involved than swapping a tank. A whole-home gas unit can demand several times the gas flow of a standard tank, and running it on an undersized line causes low flame, error codes, and lukewarm water. Venting is equally specific: condensing units use plastic vent pipe and need a condensate drain, non-condensing units need stainless steel, and neither can simply reuse an existing tank vent in most cases. Electric tankless can require several dedicated high-amperage breakers, and mistakes with gas, venting, or high-amperage wiring carry real fire and carbon monoxide risks.

Our job is to make tankless work the way it is supposed to. That starts with sizing for your busiest hour and coldest water, then confirming your gas, venting, and electrical can support the unit. We install with service valves for descaling, proper relief valve discharge, and venting built to the manual, then run the unit at full flow before we leave. If a tankless unit is not the best choice for your home, because of cost, supply limits, or how you use hot water, we say so and help you compare other options honestly.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Here's where to begin:

  • Consider a water softener or scale-prevention filter if your water is hard
  • Clean the inlet water filter screen a few times a year
  • Avoid cranking the temperature higher than you need; about 120F works for most homes
  • Protect outdoor or garage units from freezing, and keep power on during cold snaps so freeze protection works
  • If the unit shows a recurring code, note it and have it checked
  • Keep the air intake and vent termination clear of snow, leaves, and debris
  • Plan on yearly descaling through the service valves, sooner if your water is hard
