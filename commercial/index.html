Kitchen Hot Water

Restaurant and Food Service Water Heaters

In a restaurant, the dish machine, the sinks, and the handwashing stations all pull from the same supply, and a heater that's undersized or failing shows up first as a dish machine that can't hit temperature and a backed-up dish room. A high-temperature dish machine typically needs final rinse water near 180°F, which almost always calls for a booster heater working alongside the main system. We handle high-input tanks, tankless racks, booster heaters, and peak-demand sizing, and we schedule every visit around your prep and service times.

Many restaurant inspection problems trace back to water temperature. If the main heater falls behind, handwashing sinks go lukewarm, the three-compartment sink can't hold its wash temperature, and the dish machine struggles to sanitize at peak service. We verify temperatures at the tank, the booster, and the fixtures and document every visit in written reports that inspectors and landlords can review.

Equipment replacements and maintenance schedules are planned with owners, managers, and kitchen leads, so everything fits your operating schedule. If a job can't be done during service, we do it after close, so hot water is ready when the first shift arrives.

Safety

Safe Installations, Documented Service

A poorly installed or neglected water heater can lead to carbon monoxide hazards, scalding, bacterial growth in lukewarm storage, and expensive water damage from a ruptured tank. Those are real risks for a commercial property owner with employees, customers, and tenants in the building. Every installation and inspection we perform covers venting, combustion air, gas line sizing, relief valve discharge, expansion tanks, mixing valves, and code items like drain pans and seismic straps where required, and each visit ends with a written report listing risks, priorities, and recommended preventive steps.

The paperwork matters as much as the repair. Every account gets a service binder with detailed service logs, temperature readings, and relief valve test results, so you can demonstrate due diligence to landlords, insurers, and inspectors. Heavy-use equipment, in kitchens, laundries, and central plant rooms, is put on a more frequent visit schedule.
