How Alabaster Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

A little routine care is the difference between a water heater that runs for years and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode inspection every few years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a temperature setting around 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular maintenance, a standard tank typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like discolored hot water, popping noises, or a damp spot under the tank, it's time to plan a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule is cheaper and causes far less stress than an emergency swap with water spreading across the floor.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A tank that's just begun to rumble needs only a flush, yet that same buildup, neglected through a few more years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and burn out the lower element on an electric model, forcing a replacement much sooner. Save our number in your contacts today so help is one call away the moment something sounds or looks wrong. A quick call saves time, money, and ruined floors.
