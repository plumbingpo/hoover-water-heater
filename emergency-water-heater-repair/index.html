Warning Signs to Watch For

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • A puddle under the tank that keeps growing
  • Hot water far hotter than normal, a sign of a runaway thermostat or gas control
  • A complete loss of hot water throughout the house
  • A T&P valve that releases water repeatedly instead of staying closed
  • Loud popping, banging, or rumbling from the tank that has gotten worse quickly
  • Black soot or heat discoloration around the burner door or draft hood
  • A heater breaker that will not stay on after being reset
  • A rotten-egg smell near a gas water heater (leave first, and call the gas utility)

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Emergency calls combine electricity, gas, heat, pressure, and water, which is exactly the mix that makes do-it-yourself work risky. An electric heater sits on a 240-volt circuit that should never be handled while standing in water. A gas heater has fuel piping, a burner, and a vent that can spill combustion gases if something is out of place. The tank itself holds scalding water under pressure, and a discharging relief valve is signaling an unsafe condition that must be solved, not silenced. Blocking the discharge pipe, relighting next to a gas odor, or forcing a rusted connection are all common and dangerous mistakes.

A trained technician brings the right order of operations and a truck full of the likely parts. Our team confirms the heater is isolated, tests for gas leaks with a detector and soap solution rather than guessing, checks household water pressure and thermal expansion when a relief valve has been discharging, and inspects the vent for proper draft before a gas unit is restarted. Equally important, we tell you honestly when a heater can be repaired and when it cannot. A leaking seam or rusted-through bottom cannot be fixed, while a failed valve, fitting, or element usually can, and the difference is often a modest repair bill versus the cost of a whole new unit. Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Keep boxes, paint, gasoline, and cleaning products well away from a gas water heater
  • Tag the water heater supply valve and the main shutoff so nobody has to search during a leak
  • Lift the relief valve lever once a year to confirm it opens and reseats, and call if it keeps dripping
  • Add a battery leak alarm or an automatic shutoff valve that closes the supply when it senses water
  • Flush sediment from the tank yearly, since buildup overheats the bottom and speeds corrosion
  • A 120F setting is safer for skin and easier on the heater than the hottest setting
  • Put the heater in a drain pan piped to a floor drain or the exterior where the setup allows
